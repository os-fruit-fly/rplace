A1 · Reading: community notes

Read each community on its own terms

Before it looks for patterns across communities, the aggregator reads each one and checks what the evidence it was given can support. On this site, a community is one subreddit read as a case; the run's files call it a room.

Where this stage starts

Community coding has produced first-order codes tied to participants' words, second-order themes that group those codes, and a written account of each community. Reply trees record who answered whom. The aggregator receives this coded collection as the community coders sealed it, so checking the collection's coverage is part of the analysis.

What the aggregator does

First, the aggregator checks the package: whether files and counts agree, whether dates make sense, and whether missing material or work on other platforms could change an interpretation. A community that tried and failed to organize stays in the analysis. Dropping it because it left no lasting artwork would hide part of the phenomenon.

The aggregator declares a reading order and its expectations, then reads each community as a whole. Participants' words come first, the community coder's account second and canvas information last. This order separates what the record shows from what another agent has already interpreted.

Each community gets a note on its aims, its sequence of events, its uncertainties and its last observed coordination. A later claim cites the source material, and the note only gives it context. Silence at the end of a thread leaves open whether organizing ended, above all when members moved to another platform.

What the next stage receives

B1 receives a reading of every community, a declared analysis set and the recorded uncertainties. From these it builds categories that work across cases.

What the output files contain

Markdown files hold prose; JSONL files hold one structured record per line.

Community reading notesrooms/<sub>.md, <sub>, order-A1.txt

rooms/<sub>.md records each community's story and uncertainties. <sub> stands for its subreddit name. order-A1.txt records the order of reading, so later readers can see how the aggregator met the cases.

Words, patterns and possible connectionslexicon-A1.md, running-A1.md, leads-A1.jsonl

lexicon-A1.md keeps participants' vocabulary with source references. running-A1.md collects observations across readings. leads-A1.jsonl records possible connections between communities that still need checking.

The planned comparison and exclusionscontrast.md, dropped.jsonl

contrast.md declares the early-versus-late comparison and the expectations written before the reading. dropped.jsonl names each exclusion and its reason; an empty file means none were recorded.

How the end of organizing is observedcoordinating-rule-A1.md, organizing_end-A1.jsonl

coordinating-rule-A1.md sets out how coordination is told apart from recounting or joking. organizing_end-A1.jsonl records the last observed coordination and what remained, including cases where the record is silent.

Checks, departures and the handoffnotes-A1.md, defects.jsonl, departures.jsonl, run_manifest.json, handback-A1.md, _head.md

notes-A1.md explains decisions and limits. defects.jsonl records problems with the data or tools; departures.jsonl records departures from the protocol. run_manifest.json records inputs and model settings. handback-A1.md summarizes the handoff, and _head.md records the current position for continuation.
