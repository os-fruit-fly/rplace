B2 · Coding: pilot

Test whether others can use the scheme

A category can seem clear to its author and confuse everyone else. B2 tests the scheme on a small set before it is used across the whole collection.

Where this stage starts

B1 has supplied provisional categories and rules for assigning material to them. B2 starts with those rules, known confusing pairs, and a mix of difficult and randomly drawn examples. A placement assigns a second-order theme to one or more of the common categories.

What the aggregator does

The author records its own answers before it sees anyone else's. Fresh readers then receive the scheme and examples, without the author's working notes or answer key. Three readers examine each pilot item, and their replies show where the rules lead to different readings.

The aggregator asks why each important disagreement arose. Was a rule vague? Did one second-order theme bundle several kinds of action? Did a reader miss evidence? Was the author's first answer wrong? Revisions answer these causes, whatever they do to the agreement score. Each revision to the scheme has a recorded reason.

The pilot also tests whether small changes in the wording of the instructions alter placements, and whether readers answer differently at the start and the end of a batch. The results set how much material later readers receive together.

Rounds stop under the declared rule: no further edit, the declared agreement threshold, or the four-round limit. Any remaining weakness stays in the report. A separate set of reference answers then checks the frozen scheme. These answers are the author's judgments, which the files call "gold" answers. Agreement measures how consistently readers use the scheme; whether its readings are right is a separate question.

What the next stage receives

B3 receives one fixed version of the scheme, a reply format and evidence on batch design. With the rules frozen, disagreements in the full reading can be compared under the same instructions. Later stages can still revise the rules.

What the output files contain

Open the saved files from stage B2.

Markdown files hold prose; JSONL files hold one structured record per line.

The reference answersauthor_items.jsonl, gold_items.jsonl

author_items.jsonl records the author's pilot placements before comparison. gold_items.jsonl holds the separate reference items and judgments used to assess the frozen scheme as a declared standard of comparison.

The reader instructionsRETURN-B2.md

RETURN-B2.md defines what a reader must return. It makes replies comparable and lets the handler tell an incomplete reply from a real disagreement.

What each round changedpilot_rounds.jsonl, scheme_edits.jsonl, exemplar_candidates.jsonl

pilot_rounds.jsonl records the rounds and their results. scheme_edits.jsonl links each rule change to its reason. exemplar_candidates.jsonl lists difficult examples that might help readers in the full reading.

Stability and the pilot reportstability-B2.md, pilot_report.md, notes-B2.md

stability-B2.md reports how placements changed with the wording. pilot_report.md gathers agreement, reference matching, remaining confusions and practical limits. notes-B2.md records settings, thresholds and decisions.

The frozen scheme and its provenancescheme_final.md, scheme_final.jsonl, run_manifest.json

scheme_final.md is the readable scheme; scheme_final.jsonl is the same scheme as a structured record for tools. The stage's run_manifest.json entry records how the pilot was run, so later results can be tied to the tested setup.
